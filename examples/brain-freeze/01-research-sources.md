## Source 1 — Harvard Health (2026)

**Title:** What happens during a brain freeze and is it harmful?

**URL:** https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/what-happens-during-a-brain-freeze-and-is-it-harmful

### Useful facts

- Brain freeze is also called a cold-stimulus headache.
- Pain usually hits the forehead or temples.
- It typically lasts less than two minutes.
- The exact cause is debated.
- A leading explanation is that sudden cold at the roof of the mouth/back of the throat causes blood vessels to tighten and then rapidly reopen.
- Nearby pain receptors send signals through the trigeminal nerve.
- The brain can interpret that pain as coming from the head rather than the mouth: referred pain.
- It is generally harmless.
- Eating very cold food slowly is a practical prevention strategy.

## Source 2 — Cleveland Clinic

**Title:** Brain Freeze: what it is, symptoms, treatment

**URL:** https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21478-brain-freeze

### Useful facts

- Brain freeze is brief but can feel intense.
- Very cold food, drinks or even cold air can trigger it.
- Rapid changes in blood-vessel size are associated with the pain response.
- Warming the mouth/throat back toward normal temperature may help.
- Pressing the tongue against the roof of the mouth is one practical method.

## Source 3 — Mayo Clinic News Network

**Title:** Mayo Clinic Minute: Ice cream brain freeze, it's a real thing

**URL:** https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-minute-ice-cream-brain-freeze-its-a-real-thing/

### Useful facts

- Doctors describe it as a cold-stimulus headache.
- Rapid exposure to very cold substances can cause vessels in the mouth/back of the throat to change size quickly.
- That rapid change can activate pain receptors.
- The pain is intense but typically short-lived and not dangerous.
- Slower consumption is recommended to avoid triggering it.

## Cross-source agreement

The three sources broadly agree that:

1. very cold food or drink can trigger the headache quickly;
2. rapid vascular/temperature changes in the mouth or throat are involved in the leading explanation;
3. pain signaling involves nerves that make the pain feel as if it is in the head;
4. the event is brief and generally harmless;
5. slowing down and warming the roof of the mouth are reasonable practical responses.

## Important uncertainty

Do **not** write: “Scientists know exactly why brain freeze happens.”

The exact mechanism is still debated. The video can visualize the leading vessel/trigeminal-nerve explanation, but the narration should identify it as the leading explanation rather than settled fact.
